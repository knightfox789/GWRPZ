01 · Field problem

A recharge decision begins with the landscape

What would I want to know before recommending a structure here?

Two nearby locations can differ in aquifer setting, slope, runoff behaviour, land cover, drainage position and constructability. GIS brings those differences into one spatial view and helps focus the field investigation.

Practitioner note
A high-potential location becomes a candidate for closer investigation. Field conditions, the intended function of the structure, community knowledge and engineering feasibility shape the next decision.
02 · Sabarkantha case

Sabarkantha gives us a real set of sites to examine

The analysed intervention dataset covers 58 constructed structures across 49 villages. These locations let us compare mapped recharge potential with drainage position, structural context and field implementation experience.

03 · Practitioner lens

Read the landscape before choosing the structure

Ridge-to-valley position, runoff pathways, slope, weathering, drainage hierarchy, land use, existing water bodies and local observations shape the questions that should be asked before a model is opened.

Field logic first.
The map becomes more useful when the field questions are already good.
04 · Spatial evidence

Turn field questions into five spatial evidence layers

30%Aquifer
20%Slope
20%Drainage density
15%Lineament density
15%LULC

Each layer represents a field question about groundwater storage, runoff, infiltration or structural setting. Together they provide a consistent way to compare conditions across the landscape.

05 · GIS-MCDA

MCDA makes the judgement explicit

Weighted model: 0.30 Aquifer + 0.20 Slope + 0.20 Drainage Density + 0.15 Lineament Density + 0.15 LULC.

The weights record how the five evidence layers were combined. The value of MCDA is that these assumptions can be seen, checked and revisited as better local evidence becomes available.

Research context
Managed-aquifer-recharge studies use different criteria and weights because hydrogeology, intervention type and planning context vary from place to place. The method therefore needs a clear local rationale.
06 · Potential map

GWRPZ narrows the search area

The final GWRPZ map groups the landscape into Low, Moderate and High relative recharge-potential classes. In the planning process, this becomes a screening layer for deciding where closer field investigation is worthwhile.

Method note

The supplied final GWRPZ raster remains the class reference. Reconstruction of the preserved five-input workflow reproduced the upper class transition approximately, while the complete historical Class 1/2/3 break rule remains unresolved.

07 · Hydrological position

Drainage position changes the meaning of a potential zone

34Order 1
12Order 2
79.3%Order 1–2

For each structure, the analysis takes the order of the nearest mapped drainage segment and retains the point-to-line distance. Forty-six of the 58 structures are associated with Order 1–2 drainage; the median distance to the mapped network is 41.7 m.

How stream order was assigned

Structure points and drainage lines come from separate mapped datasets, so the analysis uses a reproducible nearest-network relationship and retains the distance for interpretation.

08 · Structural context

Use lineaments to guide where to investigate

27.4 mclosest mapped lineament
1,209.5 mmedian distance
5,177.5 mfarthest distance

Mapped lineaments can indicate structural discontinuities such as fractures or fault zones. Where these features are open and hydraulically connected, they can provide preferential pathways for groundwater movement and recharge. Their value in planning is to highlight structural zones that deserve closer field investigation around a candidate site.

Hydrogeological judgement matters.
A site may lie beside a mapped lineament rather than directly on it. Field observation and subsurface understanding determine whether the nearby structural feature is relevant to recharge at that location.
Method note

In the supplied geometries, none of the 58 intervention points coincides exactly with a lineament polyline. The analysis therefore uses nearest-lineament distance as the reproducible spatial relationship, without imposing an undocumented proximity threshold.

09 · Existing structures

The 58 structures show how the evidence comes together

26Moderate GWRPZ
32High GWRPZ
0Low GWRPZ

Combining GWRPZ with nearest stream order places 46 structures in an upper-catchment recharge setting, 5 in a storage-oriented moderate-potential setting and 7 in higher-order high-potential settings requiring review.

These settings are used to compare siting context. Field observations and monitoring evidence are considered separately when discussing outcomes.
