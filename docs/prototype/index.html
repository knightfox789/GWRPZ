A map can narrow the search. The field completes the decision.

How GIS, MCDA, hydrogeological reasoning, field knowledge, engineering judgement and community-informed planning can work together for groundwater recharge.

What this is
An interactive evidence story and decision guide—not a universal siting recipe.

What this is not
A claim that a high-potential GIS pixel is automatically a suitable construction site.

01 · Field problem

A recharge map is not yet a recharge decision

What would I want to know before recommending a structure here?

Two locations can sit close together and still differ in aquifer setting, slope, runoff behaviour, land cover, drainage position and constructability. GIS helps make those differences visible across a landscape.

Practitioner note
A high-potential pixel can narrow the search. It cannot tell us whether the intended structure is technically appropriate, buildable or locally acceptable.
02 · Sabarkantha case

Use the real structures to interrogate the planning logic

The validated case dataset contains 58 structures across 49 villages. They were constructed on the ground through implementation processes that included community consultation and approval.

03 · Practitioner lens

Read the landscape before choosing the structure

Ridge-to-valley position, runoff pathways, slope, weathering, drainage hierarchy, land use, existing water bodies and local observations shape the questions that should be asked before a model is opened.

Field logic first.
The map becomes more useful when the field questions are already good.

05 · GIS-MCDA

MCDA structures judgement. It does not remove judgement.

Weighted model: 0.30 Aquifer + 0.20 Slope + 0.20 Drainage Density + 0.15 Lineament Density + 0.15 LULC.

MCDA makes assumptions visible: what matters, how it is scored and how strongly it contributes. Those assumptions still need technical reasoning and context.

Research bridge
GIS-MCDA and managed-aquifer-recharge literature shows wide variation in criteria, weighting and validation approaches. That is a reason for transparency—not for copying one model everywhere.
06 · Potential map

Read GWRPZ as potential, not permission

The supplied final GWRPZ raster is used as the final class reference. Low, Moderate and High describe relative recharge potential.

Important limitation
The complete historical Class 1/2/3 break rule could not be uniquely reconstructed from the preserved workflow. The article should say that plainly.

Under the reproducible nearest-network rule, 46 of 58 structures are associated with Order 1–2 drainage. Nearest mapped stream distance ranges from 1.3 to 986.2 m, with a median of 41.7 m.

No structure point literally intersects the supplied drainage lines. The correct wording is nearest mapped stream order.
08 · Structural context

A lineament is not a yes/no answer

0/58direct intersections
27.4 mminimum distance
1,209.5 mmedian distance

The reproducible relationship is distance to the nearest mapped lineament. Distance alone does not prove active fracture connectivity, transmissivity, recharge pathway or structure performance.

Hydrogeological judgement matters.
A GIS overlay can indicate where to investigate. It cannot replace fracture verification in the field.
09 · Existing structures

What do the 58 constructed structures reveal?

A conservative combined spatial-setting summary places 46 structures in an upper-catchment recharge setting, 5 in a storage-oriented moderate-potential setting and 7 in higher-order high-potential settings requiring review.

These categories describe spatial context. They are not a performance score or engineering approval.
10 · Decision

Bring the map back to the field

Screen → Read → Interpret → Match → Consult → Verify → Engineer → Decide.

Community consultation, local knowledge, land/access, site feasibility and approval belong in the decision pathway alongside spatial evidence.

Closing principle
Use GIS to make the field visit smarter—not to eliminate the field visit.

About the work behind the article

Kaushal Gadariya is a Soil and Water Conservation Engineer working across groundwater, watershed and natural-resource management. His postgraduate research examined identification of potential sites for groundwater recharge structures using MCDA, remote sensing and GIS. He has continued applying and refining this field–GIS decision approach in professional work since 2019.

Research-grounded. Field-linked. Decision-oriented.
