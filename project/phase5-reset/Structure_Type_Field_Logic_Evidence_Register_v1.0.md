## Evidence labels

- **Field-confirmed** — practitioner logic supplied directly by Kaushal Gadariya.
- **GIS-supported** — directly supported by validated structure / GWRPZ / drainage evidence.
- **Analytical inference** — reasonable interpretation from the evidence, but not a substitute for original design / field records.
- **Open** — requires additional project documentation or field evidence.

## 1. Recharge Borewell — 43 structures

### Field-confirmed logic
Recharge borewells were proposed mainly in **community ponds with excessive runoff availability**, particularly ponds that regularly overflowed. These sites were selected through a combination of:
- GIS screening;
- field inputs;
- community consultation.

The engineering intent was to **tap excess pond overflow / captured runoff and move part of that water into the subsurface for recharge**.

### GIS-supported evidence
Recharge Borewell is the dominant intervention type: **43 of 58 structures**.

Within the validated structure set:
- 26 are nearest to Order 1 streams;
- 10 to Order 2;
- 4 to Order 3;
- 3 to Order 4.

Nearest mapped stream distance for Recharge Borewells:
- minimum 1.3 m;
- median 41.5 m;
- maximum 971.8 m.

### Interpretation
The lower-order drainage concentration is spatially consistent with the stated recharge objective: intercepting / using runoff in recharge-oriented parts of the landscape.

### Open / do not infer automatically
GIS alone does not prove that every borewell pond had excess overflow. That site-specific rationale comes from field knowledge / project records.

## 2. RRWHS — 6 structures

### Field-confirmed logic
RRWHS sites were chosen in **village school premises** because:
- the school provided a large roof catchment area;
- an existing dry open well was available nearby;
- rooftop runoff could be routed into the dry well for recharge.

### GIS-supported evidence
The six RRWHS locations are spatially more variable relative to drainage than stream-based structures:
- nearest-stream distances range from about 101 m to 986 m;
- the median is about 359 m.

### Interpretation
This supports a different siting logic from nala / checkdam interventions. RRWHS depends primarily on the **built catchment + recharge receptor (dry open well)** rather than proximity to a stream channel.

### Open
Roof area, well dimensions and recharge capacity are not in the current GIS evidence package and should come from field / design records if shown.

## 3. Pond Deepening / Widening — 4 structures

### Field-confirmed logic
The purpose was **not desilting to restore lost capacity**.

The intervention involved **pond deepening / widening to increase runoff-capture volume**, allowing more runoff to be detained and increasing the opportunity for recharge.

### GIS-supported evidence
Four Pond Deepening locations are present:
- 3 nearest Order 1 streams;
- 1 nearest Order 2 stream.

All four are therefore associated with lower-order drainage in the validated nearest-network assignment.

### Interpretation
The spatial pattern is consistent with a recharge-oriented runoff-capture strategy in upper / lower-order catchment settings.

### Open
Actual additional excavation volume and retained-water duration require engineering / monitoring records.

## 4. Checkdam / Nala Deepening-Widening — 3 Checkdam Desilting records

### Field-confirmed logic
The intervention logic was **nala deepening / widening to increase runoff-capture volume and recharge opportunity**.

The dataset label is `Checkdam Desilting`, but the practitioner clarification is that the work should not be described primarily as “removing silted storage loss”.

### GIS-supported evidence
The three validated Checkdam Desilting locations are very close to mapped drainage:
- Eklara — nearest Order 2, about 21.7 m;
- Virpura — nearest Order 3, about 4.9 m;
- Sardarpura — nearest Order 4, about 18.2 m.

Their GWRPZ context:
- Eklara — High;
- Virpura — High;
- Sardarpura — Moderate.

### Interpretation
The GIS strongly supports that these are **drainage-corridor interventions** intended to work with runoff moving through the nala system.

Two of the three occur beside Order 2–3 drainage, directly consistent with the recharge-zone logic supplied by the practitioner. The Sardarpura Order 4 example represents a more downstream / storage-oriented context and should be discussed separately rather than forced into the same recharge-zone interpretation.

### Important naming note
The public article may use a more descriptive phrase such as:
**Checkdam / Nala Deepening-Widening**
while retaining `Checkdam Desilting` in the technical data / method note for source traceability.

## 5. New Checkdam — 1 structure

### Field-confirmed logic
The intended function was **recharge + storage**.

### GIS-supported evidence
The New Checkdam at Davad:
- is in Moderate GWRPZ;
- is nearest to an Order 1 drainage line;
- lies about 6.0 m from that mapped drainage;
- falls in the recalculated Upper-catchment recharge setting.

### Analytical inference
The spatial evidence is strongly consistent with selecting a site on a small drainage corridor where runoff could be detained, creating both temporary storage and recharge opportunity.

This is an inference from the GIS plus the field-confirmed function. The exact field reason for the specific cross-section—such as nala geometry, foundation condition, available width, land / access or local demand—cannot be recovered from the current GIS layers alone.

### Open
Original survey / design / community-selection records would be needed to explain the exact site choice beyond the spatial context.

# 7. Cross-cutting drainage interpretation

Across all 58 structures:
- Order 1 — 34
- Order 2 — 12
- Order 3 — 6
- Order 4 — 6

Therefore **52 of 58 structures (89.7%)** are associated with nearest mapped stream Orders 1–3.

### Field-confirmed planning logic
The practitioner rationale is that Orders 1–3 represent recharge-oriented parts of the drainage system, and the objective was to **tap excessive runoff and create additional opportunity for groundwater recharge**.

### Article interpretation
Use the lower-order drainage concentration as a strong spatial pattern supporting the overall recharge-planning philosophy.

Do not imply that every structure was selected because of stream order alone:
- RRWHS follows roof + dry-well logic;
- pond / borewell sites also depended on runoff availability and field / community inputs;
- the final intervention depended on site-specific engineering conditions.

# 8. Recommended public story frame

The article should introduce this distinction:

## **The map helped identify where to look. The structure type depended on what the site could do.**

Then show five main implementation pathways:

1. **Community pond + excess overflow → Recharge Borewell**
2. **Large school roof + dry open well → RRWHS**
3. **Pond runoff-capture opportunity → Pond Deepening / Widening**
4. **Nala runoff-capture opportunity → Checkdam / Nala Deepening-Widening**
5. **Suitable drainage cross-section + recharge/storage purpose → New Checkdam**

This is the clearest bridge between GIS evidence and soil-and-water-conservation engineering judgement.
