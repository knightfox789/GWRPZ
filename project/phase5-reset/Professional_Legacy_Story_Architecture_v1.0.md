## 1. Why Phase 5 has been reset

The earlier Phase 5 prototypes gradually became an evidence-based decision guide with a consultancy-facing UX.

That direction was useful, but it did not fully express the original and deeper purpose of this project:

> **to present a long-running body of professional work in groundwater recharge planning as evidence of applied technical practice — from research and GIS analysis to field interpretation, community consultation, constructed interventions and professional learning.**

The article should therefore be designed first as a **professional evidence story**, and only second as a guide.

The reader should not feel that they have opened:
- a GIS tutorial;
- a project brochure;
- a donor case study;
- a consultancy landing page;
- a dashboard with explanatory cards.

The reader should feel that they are moving through the development of a technical practice.

The methodology moved into real programme landscapes.

The current Sabarkantha evidence includes:
- 58 validated constructed structures;
- 49 villages represented;
- actual field implementation;
- community consultation and approval as part of implementation;
- spatial evidence that can now be examined against constructed locations.

This is where the article shifts from “potential mapping” into applied professional practice.

---

## Layer D — Re-analysis and learning

The current project has gone back to the GIS evidence and rebuilt the methodology with stricter QA:
- preserved source data;

- documented model weights;
- common-grid analysis;
- final-raster comparison;
- structure-level GWRPZ sampling;
- nearest-stream analysis;
- lineament-distance analysis;
- explicit uncertainty.

This is not a weakness in the story.

It shows professional maturity: the work is revisited, tested and refined rather than simply repeated.

# 5. New narrative structure

The public story should have **eight major movements**.

The article does not need to number them visually as “Chapter 1, Chapter 2...” unless the final design benefits from it.

---

## Movement 1 — The question that stayed with me

### Purpose
Introduce the technical question as a long-term professional inquiry.

### Reader feeling
“This is a subject the author has worked with over time, not a one-off web article.”

### Narrative direction

Suggested opening:

> My work on groundwater recharge potential mapping began as a postgraduate research question: how can remote sensing, GIS and multi-criteria analysis help identify better locations for recharge structures? Since 2019, the question has moved with me from research into field planning, community consultation and construction. Sabarkantha provides an opportunity to bring that journey together.

### Key proposition

> **GIS helps validate the evidence. The decision still has to be made in the field, with the community.**

### Avoid
- starting with generic groundwater scarcity;
- starting with an instructional list;
- starting with “what this article is / is not”;
- consultancy messaging.

---

## Movement 2 — From potential mapping to field practice

### Purpose
Show evolution rather than immediately teaching MCDA.

### Story elements
- postgraduate research origin;
- earlier GWRPZ guide / manual as a visible artifact;
- continuing work since 2019;
- progression from maps to field questions.

### Main point

Early recharge-potential mapping helped organise the landscape.

Field application added harder questions:
- What does the aquifer actually look like at the site?
- Where is runoff moving?
- What is the local drainage position?
- Is there a weathered or fractured zone?
- What structure function is required?
- What does the community know about the site?
- Is the location technically and socially workable?

### Transition
This leads into Sabarkantha as the applied study landscape.

---

## Movement 3 — Sabarkantha as the working landscape

### Purpose
Establish place, scale and implementation reality.

### Show
- study-area map;
- villages;
- drainage;
- intervention locations;
- selected field / structure photographs when available.

### Evidence
- 58 validated constructed structures;
- 49 villages represented.

### Narrative direction

Do not describe Sabarkantha merely as a “case dataset”.

Use:

> **The landscape became a place to test how spatial evidence and field decisions come together.**

### Community thread
Introduce consultation and approval here, but do not yet turn it into a process diagram.

The reader should understand that these are constructed interventions in real village landscapes.

---

## Movement 4 — Building the spatial evidence

### Purpose
Show how an engineer turns a field question into mappable evidence.

The five-factor model becomes the central analytical sequence:

| Evidence layer | Weight | Professional question |
|---|---:|---|
| Aquifer | 30% | What subsurface setting may support storage and movement? |
| Slope | 20% | How quickly is runoff likely to move across the surface? |
| Drainage density | 20% | How dissected is the landscape and what does it suggest about runoff / infiltration behaviour? |
| Lineament density | 15% | Where does mapped structural complexity suggest zones worth hydrogeological investigation? |
| LULC | 15% | How does the surface condition influence runoff, infiltration and practical intervention context? |

### Main message

The importance is not the GIS layer itself.

The importance is the **field question represented by the layer**.

---

## Movement 5 — From five layers to recharge potential

### Purpose
Show the MCDA logic clearly and elegantly.

### Formula

`0.30 Aquifer + 0.20 Slope + 0.20 Drainage Density + 0.15 Lineament Density + 0.15 LULC`

### Story
The weighted model converts several lines of evidence into a spatial screening surface.

Then reveal the supplied final GWRPZ.

### Interpretation
Low, Moderate and High describe relative recharge potential.

The historical class-break uncertainty belongs in a compact methodology note, not in the visual foreground.

### Professional point

MCDA is valuable because it makes assumptions explicit:
- which factors are considered;
- how they are scored;
- how strongly they contribute.

That allows the model to be questioned and improved.

---

## Movement 6 — When the map meets the ground

### Purpose
This should be the analytical centre of the article.

The 58 constructed structures are brought back onto the spatial evidence.

### Part A — GWRPZ

At the 58 validated structure locations:
- Moderate: 26;
- High: 32;
- Low: 0.

Describe this as the mapped recharge-potential context of constructed structures.

---

### Part B — Hydrological position

Nearest mapped stream order:

- Order 4: 6.

Orders 1–2:
- 46 of 58;

The story should interpret the position in the drainage system rather than foregrounding GIS geometry limitations.

Technical assignment details sit in Method.

---

### Part C — Structural / lineament context

Lineaments should be explained hydrogeologically.

Use the logic:

> Mapped lineaments can represent faults, fractures or structural zones. Where these features are open, weathered and hydraulically connected, they may provide preferential pathways for groundwater movement and recharge. Their value in planning is therefore to guide where subsurface structural conditions deserve closer field investigation.

Distance from structures to nearest mapped lineament:
- minimum 27.4 m;
- median 1,209.5 m;
- maximum 5,177.5 m.

The analytical finding that none of the point geometries exactly intersects a mapped lineament remains in Method, not as the headline.

### Visual goal
The reader should understand the subsurface logic, not merely see a distance statistic.

---

## Movement 7 — From spatial evidence to a constructed intervention

### Purpose
Show professional practice beyond GIS.

The sequence becomes:

**Spatial screening**
→ **field reconnaissance**
→ **local / community knowledge**
→ **site discussion**
→ **land / access / feasibility**
→ **technical design**
→ **community / institutional approval**
→ **construction**
→ **observation / monitoring**

### Main message

The final decision belongs to a combined technical and community process.

### Practitioner quote

> **GIS helps validate the evidence. The decision still has to be made in the field, with the community.**

### Required visual evidence
Where available, use real photographs of:
- field reconnaissance;
- community consultation;
- structure site;
- completed intervention;
- monitoring / post-construction condition.

Do not replace real practice with generic iconography if real evidence exists.

---

## Movement 8 — What this body of work has taught me

### Purpose
End with professional synthesis rather than “key takeaways”.

The closing should sound reflective, technical and field-grounded.

Possible structure:

### 1. Potential mapping is useful when it narrows a real field question.
### 2. Hydrological position changes how a candidate location is interpreted.
### 3. Structural evidence is most useful when linked back to hydrogeology.
### 4. A spatial model becomes more valuable when assumptions remain visible.
### 5. Community knowledge and technical feasibility shape what is actually built.
### 6. Re-analysis is part of professional practice; methods improve when evidence is revisited.

### Closing sentence direction

> The value of GIS in groundwater recharge planning is not that it replaces judgement. It is that it helps make the evidence behind that judgement visible.

# 10. Editorial rules

The final public article should avoid:
- “this is not...” repetition;
- defensive disclaimer boxes;
- formulaic AI contrasts;
- excessive card layouts;
- consultancy language in the hero;
- generic motivational language;
- overstating outcomes;
- “expert” labels.

Preferred writing rhythm:

**Observation → technical meaning → field implication**

Use limitations where they materially change interpretation, but place them proportionately.
