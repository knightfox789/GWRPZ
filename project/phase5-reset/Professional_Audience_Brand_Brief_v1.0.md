# 3. Audience matrix

## A. Soil & water conservation / watershed practitioner

### Should take away
- GIS can organise landscape evidence before field planning;
- ridge-to-valley position matters;
- factors should represent real hydrological questions;
- structure planning continues beyond the suitability map.

### Evidence they need
- maps;
- drainage logic;
- structure examples;
- field workflow.

### Tone
Practical, technical, direct.

### Do not overemphasise
- GIS software mechanics;
- academic theory.

---

## B. Hydrogeologist

### Should take away
- the article understands the difference between mapped evidence and subsurface conditions;
- lineaments are interpreted as structural guides rather than binary “intersection” targets;
- aquifer / fracture / weathering interpretation belongs in field verification;
- model uncertainty is acknowledged.

### Evidence they need
- aquifer logic;
- lineament interpretation;
- structural context;
- methodology notes;
- uncertainty.

### Tone
Technically careful, hydrogeologically literate.

### Do not overclaim
- fracture connectivity;
- recharge rate;
- transmissivity;
- lineament functionality without field evidence.

---

## C. Academic / researcher

### Should take away
- the work has a clear MCDA / GIS lineage;
- assumptions and weights are visible;
- source reconstruction and QA are traceable;
- practice has fed back into method refinement.

### Evidence they need
- methodology;
- research references;
- data lineage;
- uncertainty;
- reproducibility notes.

### Tone
Transparent and evidence-led.

### Do not turn the article into
- a journal paper;
- a full methods repository.

---

## D. Student

### Should take away
- a spatial model begins with a real question;
- thematic layers represent processes;
- weighting is judgement made explicit;
- field verification remains essential.

### Evidence they need
- clear maps;
- simple factor explanation;
- conceptual diagrams.

### Tone
Accessible without oversimplification.

---

## E. CSR professional / donor

### Should take away
- GIS can improve where technical attention and investment are directed;
- investment quality depends on field verification and implementation context;
- community consultation is part of a viable intervention pathway;
- evidence can be revisited and improved.

### Evidence they need
- study scale;
- real constructed structures;
- process from screening to implementation;
- outcome evidence where available.

### Tone
Decision-oriented, transparent.

### Do not overwhelm with
- raster QA details in the main story.

---

## F. NGO / programme / government technical team

### Should take away
- the approach can support programme planning;
- local data quality matters;
- community and engineering processes are integral;
- GIS is most useful when connected to field teams.

### Evidence they need
- workflow;
- implementation sequence;
- data requirements;
- examples.

# 7. Typography and composition

Use editorial hierarchy:
- strong large headlines;
- generous white space;
- readable body text;
- map captions;
- figure labels;
- quiet method notes.

Prefer fewer, larger visual objects.

Avoid:
- rows of equal cards;
- chip/pill clutter;
- repeated alert boxes;
- multiple CTA buttons;
- heavy border-radius everywhere.

---

# 8. Voice

Use Kaushal Voice:

**Observation → technical meaning → field implication**

Examples:

Instead of:
“Lineament intersection is not required.”

Use:
“Mapped lineaments help identify structural zones that deserve closer hydrogeological investigation. Where fractures are open and connected, they may provide preferential pathways for groundwater movement.”

Instead of:
“GWRPZ is not final suitability.”

Use:
“GWRPZ narrows the landscape to areas where the recharge conditions are more favourable; field and engineering checks then determine what can actually be proposed.”

This keeps caution inside the reasoning rather than writing the article as a sequence of rebuttals.

# 12. Brand success test

After reading, each audience should remember:

1. **the technical question**;
2. **the Sabarkantha evidence**;
3. **the map-to-field logic**;
4. **the author connected to the work**.

If the reader remembers the UI more than the study, the design has failed.

If the reader remembers the author but not the evidence, the branding is too strong.

If the reader remembers the evidence but cannot tell whose body of work it represents, the authorship is too weak.
